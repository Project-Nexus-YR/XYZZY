# XYZZY Commercialization Research

## 1. Models used by comparable dev-tool/collab-infra OSS (2024-2026)

**Open-core:**
- **Cal.com**: AGPLv3 core; gates SSO/SAML/enterprise compliance behind a $30/seat Enterprise tier, and even self-hosted instances need a Commercial License for team workflows/advanced routing. Teams tier $12/user/mo unlocks team event types, round-robin, routing forms. Self-host is free but a paid license needs a 30-user minimum. Sources: https://cal.com/blog/cal-diy-open-source-to-closed-source, https://staxly.dev/platforms/cal-com
- **n8n**: "fair-code" via its own Sustainable Use License (SUL): free for internal business use and even running commercial automations yourself, but you cannot resell/rehost n8n as a service to third parties or white-label it. Enterprise contracts reported at $2,000-5,000/mo. Source: https://docs.n8n.io/sustainable-use-license/, https://openhosst.com/blog/n8n-self-hosted-pricing
- **Supabase**: Apache-2.0/MIT/PostgreSQL-licensed core, fully self-hostable (Auth, PostgREST, Storage, Realtime, Kong). Revenue comes entirely from managed cloud (Free $0, Pro $25/mo, Team $599/mo with SOC2/ISO27001, custom Enterprise). Notably turned down large enterprise contracts to preserve the open-source-aligned pricing model. Source: https://sacra.com/c/supabase/, https://automationatlas.io/answers/supabase-pricing-explained-2026/

**License-change plays (source-available / BSL / fair source):**
- **HashiCorp**: moved Terraform et al. from MPL to Business Source License (BSL 1.1) in 2023-24, restricting competitors from offering it as a competing hosted service; converts to open source after a time delay. Triggered the OpenTofu fork. Source: https://www.hashicorp.com/en/blog/hashicorp-adopts-business-source-license, https://thenewstack.io/hashicorp-abandons-open-source-for-business-source-license/
- **Sentry**: created the "Fair Source" definition/FSL: source publicly readable, usable with minimal restriction, converts to true open source (Apache-2.0) after a delay (2 years for FSL). Adopted alongside BUSL by Couchbase, CockroachDB, MariaDB. Source: https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/22/some-startups-are-going-fair-source-to-avoid-the-pitfalls-of-open-source-licensing/, https://blog.sentry.io/open-source

No public per-company revenue breakdowns beyond Supabase's pricing tiers and n8n's reported enterprise contract range were surfaced; deeper adoption/revenue figures (e.g., ARR) were not found in this pass.

## 2. What stayed open (the wedge) vs what got gated (the monetization): for team-seat buyers

- **Open/wedge across all three open-core examples**: the full single-tenant, self-hostable product with core functionality intact: Cal.com's booking engine, n8n's workflow engine (including commercial internal use), Supabase's entire backend stack. This is what drives adoption and lets an individual or small team fully evaluate the product with zero payment friction.
- **Gated/monetization, consistently team/org-scale features**: SSO/SAML and enterprise compliance (Cal.com Enterprise tier, Supabase Team tier's SOC2/ISO27001), multi-seat team collaboration features (Cal.com's team event types, round-robin, routing forms), and, critically, the *right to resell/host it for others* (n8n's SUL explicitly blocks third-party hosting/white-labeling while leaving internal commercial use free). Supabase's split is simplest: self-host is fully free, monetization is 100% in "someone else runs the infra for you" (managed cloud), not feature-gating.
- Pattern for team buyers: individual/single-org use stays free; anything that scales to *multiple teams inside one org* (SSO, compliance, advanced routing/roles) or *reselling as infra* gets gated. Source: same URLs as section 1.

## 3. License choice implications for a solo founder

- **MIT**: maximal permissiveness, no viral/copyleft protection; anyone (including a cloud vendor) can take the code and host it as a competing service with no obligation to share changes. XYZZY is currently MIT.
- **Apache-2.0**: like MIT plus explicit patent grant; Supabase uses this for its main repo. Same competitive-hosting exposure as MIT, but slightly stronger legal protection for the founder against patent claims. Source: https://sacra.com/c/supabase/
- **AGPLv3**: copyleft that extends to network use: anyone who modifies and offers the software over a network must release their modifications. Cal.com uses AGPLv3 for exactly this reason (it discourages silent competing SaaS forks), but Cal.com *still* needed a separate Commercial License layered on top for enterprise features and reportedly had to go partially closed-source for parts of its "Cal.diy" self-host offering, per its own blog, suggesting AGPL alone didn't fully solve the resale/compete problem. Source: https://cal.com/blog/cal-diy-open-source-to-closed-source
- **BSL/FSL (source-available with delayed open-sourcing)**: HashiCorp's move explicitly to stop AWS/competitors from offering hosted Terraform without contributing back; provoked a community fork (OpenTofu) and reputational backlash for changing license on an already-large open-source project. Sentry's FSL is the "gentler" version, defined specifically for this problem, with a built-in future-open-source guarantee. Source: https://thenewstack.io/hashicorp-abandons-open-source-for-business-source-license/, https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/22/some-startups-are-going-fair-source-to-avoid-the-pitfalls-of-open-source-licensing/
- n8n avoided both copyleft and BSL backlash by writing a custom permissive-but-anti-resale license (SUL) instead, a third path worth naming as precedent for a solo founder who doesn't want AGPL's viral complexity or BSL's "un-open-source" optics. Source: https://docs.n8n.io/sustainable-use-license/

## 4. Recommendation for XYZZY

**Model**: Open-core + hosted cloud (Supabase/Cal.com pattern), not a pure BSL/FSL license-change play: XYZZY is pre-launch with no adoption yet, so a HashiCorp-style change has no installed base to protect and only adds legal complexity a solo founder doesn't need yet.

**License**: Given XYZZY is a private repo about to go public and is already MIT, two reasonable paths: (a) keep MIT for adoption-first simplicity: accept that this permits competing hosted forks, matching Supabase's Apache-2.0 bet that adoption outweighs fork risk; or (b) switch to n8n-style Sustainable Use License / a simple non-compete clause before the public release if resale-by-cloud-vendors is a real founder concern, since retrofitting a copyleft license (AGPL) after external contributions exist is much harder than before. Relicensing to AGPL post-launch, per Cal.com's own experience, did not fully prevent the need for a separate commercial license anyway: so it is not obviously worth the complexity for a single-node SQLite tool at this stage.

**What to gate first** (of the five candidates): SSO/OIDC and audit/compliance exports should be the first gates: they map directly to the "team buyer, not individual" pattern seen in Cal.com's and Supabase's Enterprise tiers, and they are the features an evaluating team lead can point to when justifying a purchase. Multi-node/HA and managed hosting are natural second-stage gates (managed hosting = the actual Supabase-style revenue engine once XYZZY has users who don't want to run FastAPI+SQLite themselves). Priority model-provider integrations are the weakest gate candidate: comparable products (n8n) keep integrations open as part of the adoption wedge, not the paywall.

**Top 3 adoption prerequisites before any monetization model matters**: (1) a one-command self-host path (Docker Compose, matching Supabase's stated docker-compose self-host story), (2) public docs good enough that a team can self-evaluate without founder involvement, (3) a live public demo/room a prospect can join immediately: since XYZZY's product is inherently multiplayer, a demo that lets a stranger *feel* the multi-agent room experience is likely a stronger adoption lever here than for any of the comparables above, none of which are collaborative-by-nature in the same way. This is an inference from XYZZY's own described shape, not sourced to any external precedent.
